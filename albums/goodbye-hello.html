Content

Goodbye/Hello is the self-released album by The Luke Walton Band: Luke Francis Walton (then publicly credited as Luke Walton; not Luke Walton the basketball coach), Logan Shrewsbury (drums), Peter Lee Johnson (violin), and Nick “Thor” Campbell (bass). The group formed in USC Thornton’s inaugural Popular Music Program during the 2009–2010 school year, playing weekly around Los Angeles before a self-booked summer 2010 California coast tour (San Diego to San Francisco) with Rozzi Crane and Liplash, Walton and Crane’s first tour together. The Luke Walton Band ran one bill; Crane’s band was the same Walton lineup with Sam Wilkes on bass. On September 24, 2010, the band closed a USC Thornton night at Rusty’s Surf Ranch on the Santa Monica Pier. Neon Tommy (Cynthia Balderas, September 25) puts the Derik Nelson Band and Rozzi Crane on before them, and lists “Goodbye,” “Heart in Hand,” a “Crossroads” cover, “Stay the Same,” “A Good Girl,” “Where We’re Going To (Go),” and an acoustic Taylor Swift cover she calls “Love Song,” folded into “Hello Beautiful.” Concert Archives has that same Friday as Rozzi, luke walton, and Dainty. An earlier Rusty’s bill, May 21, 2010, is Elias, Rozzi, luke walton, and Pinhead.

Spring semester of freshman year, circa 2010, he was jamming in the dorms at Pardee Tower. The photo has no exact day.

He played the San Diego County Fair more than once in 2010. One date is acoustic, probably June. July 4, 2010 is Luke Walton and Jordan Sorokin as The Luke Walton Band, filling in. The drummer in that frame is not named. That night is not the July 4, 2011 Del Mar Fair set.

May 1, 2012. Left to right: Peter Lee Johnson, Luke Walton, Logan Shrewsbury, Rozzi Crane, Mia Minichiello, Nick Campbell. The venue is not named.

The archived discography runs Just A Friend (2007), Heart in Hand (2008), The Other Side (2009, Luke Walton featuring Blue Suburbia), then this album in 2010.

The high-school bands ran at the same time. Rock I IV V was a 2006 school club, named for the I–IV–V progression. Casey McSocial was his first band, on bass, and won the Battle of the Bands. That win is his account. Mannequin (2006–2008) was original songs he co-founded with Jonathan Gillie on guitar and Zach Freeman on drums. They came in second. That placement is his account too. Blue Suburbia (2008–2009) won, and the 2009 Coast News profile confirms that win. He did not compete as a freshman, which is how a three-band run becomes the Coast News line about winning all but one year. He remembers the competitions in the first semester and is not sure. The rounds are in the catalog. He graduated from La Costa Canyon High School in January 2009.

On the solo EPs he played guitar and piano, sang, and wrote. Stores credited him as Luke Walton. There was national radio and television, and a YouTube push from December 2008, before The Luke Walton Band put his name on the marquee.

The public break was a video on March 21, 2009. He rewrote Taylor Swift's "Love Story" to ask her for a date. It passed 300,000 views. She never answered.

In May the San Diego Union-Tribune covered encore screenings of We Must Remember, a Carlsbad High documentary about the Holocaust that he scored. The paper names him as composer and as a performer at MovieMax Carlsbad.

The San Diego Reader ran "Swift Serenade" on June 17: a recent graduate of La Costa Canyon, the YouTube rewrite, KSON and local television, and about 45,000 views at the time of writing. Six days earlier the June 11 Reader lists a Sunday acoustic bill at Swedenborg Hall, 1531 Tyler Avenue, University Heights, 7:30 p.m. Brooklyn Mack, with Jessica Lerner, Karen Real, Jordan Reimer, Sven-Erik Seaholm, and Luke Walton (screenshot). June 11 was a Thursday, so that Sunday is June 14. The June 2009 San Diego Troubadour prints the same night as Sunday the 14th (screenshot).

The video also drew NBC New York, the Orlando Sentinel, the Nashville City Paper, the San Diego Union-Tribune on July 10, a print feature in Country Weekly, and the Winter 2009 cover of USC Trojan Family Magazine, Walton with Peter Lee Johnson, Leland Cox, and Mia Minichiello (PDF). He went to Nashville in July 2009.

He graduated a semester early, in January 2009, and used the open months for a video project. "Six Months to Make It" is that project: 21 episodes, January through July 2009. Episode 1, "Driving Test," went up January 12. The Coast News covered it as a North County musician's run-up to USC and this album. One episode is getting into Thornton's first Popular Music Program.

Goodbye/Hello landed September 1, 2010 on Bandcamp, eleven tracks (~35:05 on the official site), pop rock / Los Angeles, digital album at 16-bit/44.1 kHz with per-track streaming on Bandcamp. Full per-track lyrics archive (2012 captures): Goodbye · Hello Beautiful · Stay The Same · A Good Girl · Dead End Love · Where We're Going To (Go) · Just A Friend · Heart in Hand · Who Needs Tears · Preschool · Missin' the Way. Five cuts reissued from solo EPs: Preschool, Dead End Love, and Who Needs Tears share lyrics with the 2006/2008 versions; Just A Friend and Heart in Hand were rewritten for the LP.

Preschool has a distinct earlier commercial credit: track 5 on Charitable Chords’s Know Your Rights (In Collaboration With Knit The World): June 10, 2010, billed as Luke Walton (Blue Suburbia). Jake Barnes’ student charity project (Social Justice course); proceeds to Knit The World (Sarah Edelstein). Same song, three months before the Luke Walton Band LP: Blue Suburbia name on the comp, band marquee on Goodbye/Hello.

All eleven tracks now link to Songs. Present-tense credits use Luke Francis Walton or Scoobert Doobert; the bare “Luke Walton” credit belongs to this historical window only.
