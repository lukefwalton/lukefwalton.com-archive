hostNote: |
  The last episode of 2024 is a look back at the guests, for anyone who missed one, and for the resolution I want in 2025. [Benjamin Lazar Davis](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/benjamin-lazar-davis/) did his conversation walking, with Los Angeles behind him, and I had watched him play an improvised set with Kimbra. The photo shoot and the accounting orbit the work. The music is the nucleus, and it needs tending. The resolution is to lean harder into the artist self, and to find it by going into the cave: a loose idea, the chance of missing the original goal, and an ending I am still hyped about. A brief can pay. A Coca-Cola spot that should sound like Doja Cat mixed with Snoop Dogg is a real job. The well fills when I set out with a loose conception and yes-and my way somewhere else. I thought I was referencing the Brothers Johnson. A listener, told that, would say I hadn't. [Joshoo](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/joshoo/) is the same lesson from a career I respect. Trumpet, flugelhorn, his own songs, a tour in Peter Gabriel's band, then an ambient record I did not expect. A lot of people I know, myself included, made less money this year than the year before. The gutsy record is one answer. His Apple commercial is the receipt. He was writing a song that felt deep, trying to get an emotion into the bottle, with no keywords for an agency to search.

  A clip of a Snarky Puppy member, separate from [Ryan Haft](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/ryan-haft/), made the collaboration point I keep. If the demo is too finished, the other person is stuck polishing my box, and if I was going to do all the work it should have been a solo song. Ryan's own conversation was the studio and the stage. The mix room is a sealed box and a microscope. Live sound is the same skills with a different clock, and a different room every night. [Bermuda Schwartz](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/bermuda-schwartz/) came up on diamond records by his own path, treated MTV as a technology, and is still learning after the kind of success that could have been a retirement. He will call a drummer to ask what the sample was. I think the record was Imagine Dragons. [JW Francis](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/jw-francis/) is building the tour he actually wants, in rooms that are his dream and not everyone's, because agencies and booking deals had a shape he refused. The jump from one orbit of a career to the next is where you slip back. It is usually a song, or a tour. Sabrina Carpenter is one. MUNA, who opened for Taylor Swift, is the kind of tour I mean. [Greg Byers](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/greg-byers/) made the money legible for a string player outside a big orchestra job: teach, play, change ensembles, keep the expenses down. He also got me to take the body seriously. Running, walking, grip. I am a guitarist, and hand strength is how I stay out of the overuse injuries. [Jon Sandler](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/jon-sandler/) is further up the touring ladder than I am, and the problems change shape rather than going away. He had just moved to Los Angeles. [Luke Moellman](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/luke-moellman/), from the year before, had the same antenna. Blogs and Hype Machine were a moment, and they took it. Playlists may be the current one. The readiness is the point.

  [Eliott Glinn](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/eliott-glinn/) talks about the mix the way a cinematographer talks about focus. Something is there to listen to. Something is there for vibe. They trade places. [Harley Eblen](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/harley-eblen/) started on cello, moved across the strings, and uses Melodyne to see an arrangement, beyond tuning the notes. I have been doing that with background vocals since [KŌAN](https://lukefwalton.com/albums/koan/), on [a song to quit your job to](https://lukefwalton.com/songs/a-song-to-quit-your-job-to/) and the [Wouldn't It Be Nice](https://lukefwalton.com/songs/wouldn-t-it-be-nice/) cover. The tool trains the ear, and the ear goes back into the tool. I want the writing tools to work the same way. I will ask for words associated with a theme, summer, popsicle, sunburn, and I will ask it to leave the lyric to me. A rhyme list lays out the obvious ones, and then I remember the one it would have missed. Synplant sits in the same drawer, an idea generator I am not even sure is AI. [Martin Rieger](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/martin-rieger/) is the conversation for spatial audio past one company's format, including what binaural headphones can take from a film theater. [Leah Bowden](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/leah-bowden/) describes the community side I miss when a song only goes out on the internet. Ensembles, the people in the room, the history the music came from. Pop is fluent in the cult of personality. The lineup, the lead-in, and the pictures can carry where a music came from.

  [Jesse McGinty](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/jesse-mcginty/) and I made [Michael Bublé, My Santa Buddy](https://lukefwalton.com/songs/michael-buble-my-santa-buddy/), the one where Bublé thaws out of the ice cave. His answer to why he loves music is the one I replay. Love, the way I am using it here, is the lack of perfection. It is acceptance, and it is sacrifice. That is why we renamed the show Love Music More. Somebody's answer changes my reason. My life is not the same without the music, so I have to give some back. [Part 2](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/2024-in-review-a-recap-of-my-guest-pods-pt-2/) is the rest of the year.
