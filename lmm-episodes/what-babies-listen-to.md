hostNote: |
  A 2026 study looked at babies in the first two days of life and asked whether they can already predict music. I use that to ask what the prediction says about music itself.

  The comparison is a real phrase against a scrambled one. The newborns showed stronger neural responses when an expected next step was cut off. They were forming expectations about rhythm and melody from the phrase in front of them, from its own vocabulary, not from a lifetime of songs. I had thought that kind of statistical listening showed up later, in some deeper phase of brain development. It is there on day zero.

  What I will not let the study become is a proof that music is a universal language. Consonance, an out-of-place pitch, the pleasantness people treat as physics: I do not get a clean newborn result for that. Timing and surprise light up. A brain can keep a musical context and react when the context breaks. That is the finding. It is already a lot.

  The week before, on [Fishbowls of Influence](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/fishbowls-of-influence/), the question was whether a chord is good or bad outside the culture that trained the ear. This episode is the other end of that. Before the fishbowl, is there already a listener? The week after, [Monkey Studies](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/monkey-studies/) takes the same question to rhythm, and to rhesus macaques.
