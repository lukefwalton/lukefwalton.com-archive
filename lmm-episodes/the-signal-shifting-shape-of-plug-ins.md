  I don't think music is math. I think recording is. The music is magic, and the math is how that magic gets into a speaker and stays there. I start with a sine wave. Add another note and the wave gets less smooth, more rub, more dissonance. I can change the wave shape on a synth without changing the note on the piano. Lay two signals together and the sum is a new wave. From two there is one. Harmonics are why that synth does not sound like a guitar, a violin, or an oboe. The ear hears the fingerprint, the microphone does too, and the speaker has to build it again.

  A computer cannot store a continuous wave. Cut the gap between two steps in half forever and you never arrive, so digital audio samples the wave. It is an approximation, the way a movie is frames. Sample rate and bit depth are the longer version of that, on [What is sample rate and bit depth?](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/what-is-sample-rate-and-bit-depth-and-why-does-it-matter/). Once the wave is numbers, convolution is available. An impulse response is a clap with the clap removed: an elevator, a hallway, a cathedral, outside. Apply it to a dry synth and the synth can sit in the Taj Mahal, or inside the body of an acoustic guitar. A voice convolved with a synth is the start of a vocoder, and more than that is going on. Spring, chamber, and plate are on [The Types of Reverb](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/the-types-of-reverb-with-examples/). Convolution is the one that uses the clap. That clap and its decay are the time domain. The frequency version is a Fourier transform. Run a pure sine through a preamp and the preamp leaves its character on the wave. Flip the phase and two perfectly opposed signals cancel. That is how noise-cancelling headphones work, and it is the same cancellation I use for width on [Panning, Psychedelic Effects, and Headphone Vectors](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/panning-psychedelic-effects-and-headphone-vectors/). An airplane hum is the easy case. A scream is sharp, with a lot of attack, and it comes through. Miss the flip and the math can make the noise louder.

  The same subtraction is how a plug-in learns a piece of gear. Run a sine through a circuit, including something like the Beatles' Abbey Road recording desk, subtract the sine, and keep the difference. A dying battery on a fuzz pedal is one of those differences, a nonlinear voltage, and the supply on my pedalboard has a setting that fakes the sag. A plug-in is a person who did that math and sells it inside a DAW. Sausage Fattener puts an angry mascot on a clipping circuit. Universal Audio goes into studios and tries to capture a compressor or an EQ I cannot afford, the kind of control people call more musical. Around 2012 the computers got fast enough to hear it. Early digital wanted to sound clean and leave tape behind. I put fake tape on the inserts so the music still sounds like my music, including on a plane. The longer case for that color is [Analog vs. Digital](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/analog-vs-digital-which-is-better-and-why/).

  A mix for someone who wants more sauce can stack hundreds of these moves, in frequency, in time, and in amplitude. This is the first computer I have owned that almost keeps up. Recording stays a representation of the music, not the music. The microphone is a kind of ear. The cable, the preamp, and the processing are further choices, and a choice can beat the source. If I look at those choices the way I look at a synthesizer, the waveform, how loud it is, how hard it clips, how it attacks and decays, I stop freezing. There are infinite right answers. The set of moves is small.

    answer: "I don't think so. The music is the magic. Recording is the math, the work of taking a sound off an instrument and putting a copy of it in a speaker. You need a little of that math to get the magic onto Spotify."
  - question: "What is an impulse response?"
    answer: "It is what a room does to a clap after you take the clap away. An elevator, a hallway, a cathedral, and the outdoors each leave a different decay. Convolution applies that decay to a dry sound, so a synth can sit in the Taj Mahal or inside an acoustic guitar."
  - question: "What does an audio plug-in actually do?"
    answer: "Somebody worked out the math and applied it to the signal. The picture on the screen can be a joke. Universal Audio's plug-ins are an attempt to capture a compressor or an EQ I cannot afford."
  - question: "Why do noise-cancelling headphones miss a sudden sound?"
    answer: "They play the flipped phase of a steady outside sound, and two perfectly flipped signals cancel. An airplane hum is the easy case. A scream is sharp, with a lot of attack, and it pokes through. If the flip is wrong, the math can make the noise louder instead."
