hostNote
[Part 1](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/2024-in-review-a-recap-of-my-guest-pods-pt-1/) ran long, so this is the rest of the 2024 guests, each one paused on for a lesson I want in 2025. [Aaron Comess](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/aaron-comess/) is the snare on Two Princes. A piccolo snare, tight and higher, which became a 1990s sound and sat apart from the 1980s one. The 1980s snare was big, and then gated, that clipped artificial reverb. Jack Antonoff is deep in the gated sound now. Aaron wanted the piccolo and the fill when other people in the room did not, and a lot of what came after sounds like that decision. He will not take the credit. The other half is that the song still sounds like a band. The hook is the vibe of people playing together, and the tune starts in the instrumental section. I like Maroon 5, especially the early records. I use them as the picture of a group that came to sound like a pop vocal with familiar players around it. [Matt Meyer](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/matt-meyer/) at Vintage King is the person I want in the room when the gear is a pile of numbers. A Neve 1073, a UA 1176, the LN and the AE, a FET microphone against the version you actually wanted for the vocal or the kick. Pro audio is expensive, and the marketing is a mess. He is also a voiceover artist, which I notice every time I trip on a sentence. [Qu1tter](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/qu1tter/) is the current LA studio. Lost hard drives, a prayer that the artist asks for the file you did back up, 6 a.m., and a room that wipes the drives because it turns over and nothing can leak. My hometown studio in San Diego does not work like that. The lesson is that I will make a different mistake, one that was not on the list. He is in the thick of it, with Future, GloRilla, and London on da Track, and with the same tools a home producer has open. Trackspacer, Soothe, Splice. [Andy Shernoff](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/andy-shernoff/) of the Dictators is the legend half, and the part I can use today. Early punk, CBGB, a scene that was organic and also built, from the Sex Pistols and later the Clash out to Green Day on a New Year's broadcast. That conversation sent me off to read. When I am in Tokyo with artists, watching who shares a room, I can see the early shape and ask how a scene gets heard. [Gustav Brunn](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/gustav-brunn/) is the Sweden version of support. He grew up with rooms to record in and bands to play in, and he is a working musician who has won Swedish Grammys. A community, a grant, a school studio, some pooled resource, can turn into a person like that. [Mike Post](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/mike-post/) at MooseCat is the vibe. If the record is what a band will tour for the next two to five years, the session is a stress machine: songs you will hate if you get them wrong, the click, tape or Pro Tools, who else to hire. His job is also the snack and the suggestion, the thing that gets people out of their heads and onto the instruments. He talks about Local Natives and Alice Cooper that way. [Justin Kohler](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/justin-kohler/) is the same job in Loveland, between Boulder and Fort Collins, in a house, with younger bands arriving because the rent is cheaper than Denver. Cheap rent equals good musicians. That was part of CBGB too. I am very Southern California. It helps to hear a living being made somewhere else. [Bryson Barnes](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/bryson-barnes/), head of audio at NBC News, made me nervous on purpose. I run audio at [Micasa](https://micasarecording.com/) in San Diego, and the scale is a joke I am telling on myself. Podcasting inherited radio, and radio inherited AM, which was mono. A lot of spoken word still sounds like that inheritance, lower resolution, everything in the center. He talks about slight pans, beds, and full fidelity, and he can point at Peabody wins and, as of that October, fifteen number-one Apple podcasts. I like the form. An author reading their own book has a pace a hired voice will smooth out, and a person telling the story in their own voice is richer than the page. Take the picture away and you hear the crack and the laugh, and then music can do a job it does less well under TV news. [Mike Gillilan](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/mike-gillilan/) at Giltone made mastering legible to me. Minute moves. You are paying for taste and for gear. I had been treating it as a dark art. [Jeb Sherrill](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/jeb-sherrill/) and [Lindy Day](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/lindy-day/) were the guitar pair. Jeb sees patterns and tries to get you out of the one box you keep running up and down. I know that plateau. Lindy is building the teaching in public, and playing at a level that keeps the instrument alive. [Justin Gammella](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/justin-gammella/) went from a duo and sync work into writing for other people, FINNEAS, Bella Poarch, Bahamas, and he is also on Capitol Hill making the case that songwriters get paid. If millions of people are hearing the song, the writer should be able to afford a car. The hope, in the industry conversations I am having, is thin. I feel it in my own work. I want it back in the room. [Jay Jay French](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/jay-jay-french/) is the example of pressing anyway. Twisted Sister came up gritty, and their advantage was the live show: out all the time, nothing precious, test it again. His line is that they were going to put on the best show, so they were going to win. [Stephan Hawkes](https://lukefwalton.com/love-music-more/episodes/stephan-hawkes/) closed the year with the question I want to be able to answer. Why hire me. What value do I bring. Gear, a studio, instincts, chops, and he understands mastering better than I do.

  • question
    Why does the snare on Two Princes matter?
    answer
    Aaron Comess used a piccolo snare, tight and higher, and he kept it when the room wanted something else. That sound became a 1990s waypoint, set against the big gated snare of the 1980s. Jack Antonoff is back in the gated sound now. The lesson I take is the risk. A specific decision about feel can outlast the meeting where people argued with it.
  • question
    What did the 2024 studio guests have in common?
    answer
    File hygiene, and vibe. Qu1tter's LA rooms wipe drives, and the mistake you did not plan is the one you make. Mike Post at MooseCat and Justin Kohler in Loveland are both trying to get a nervous band out of its head, in a busy commercial room or a dialed-in house. Matt Meyer is who I trust when the gear names are a pile of numbers.
  • question
    How did Twisted Sister make the live show the advantage?
    answer
    Jay Jay French is plain about it. They were going to put on the best show, and they were out there constantly, testing, with nothing held precious. The strategy was the room, every night. Stephan Hawkes, later in the episode, asks the question I want a version of: why hire me, and what value do I bring.
