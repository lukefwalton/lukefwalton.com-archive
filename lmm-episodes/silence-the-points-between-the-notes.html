hostNote
When the music stops, what is left? I start from Debussy and Miles on the spaces between notes, then **John Cage’s 4'33"** — you cannot make a silence no matter what you try. Guitarists who transcribe horn players are really learning where to breathe. Rhythm needs absence the way a phrase needs inhale. The brain still answers silence; groove is placement plus the hole that makes it funky. So much of the dopamine is anticipation, not the hit.

faq
  • question
    What is John Cage's 4'33"?
    answer
    4'33" is a composition by John Cage where performers do not play their instruments for four minutes and thirty-three seconds, inviting the audience to focus on the ambient sounds and silence.
  • question
    How does silence affect musical phrasing?
    answer
    Silence provides necessary pauses and space which allow for rhythmic articulation and emotional expression in music.
  • question
    Why is silence important in music composition?
    answer
    Silence helps create anticipation and contrast, enriching the listener's experience and enhancing the impact of musical notes.
