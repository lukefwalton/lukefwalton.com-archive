description
Groove is one of the most elusive parts of music, but it defines genre and often success. Why does some music feel good? Why do some bassists and drummers "lock"? And why is it so much harder than just clicking "quantize" on the computer? I think the secret to music's next phase is in the phase shift. AI isn't going anywhere, so we need to add more humanity to the human music. It's a weird future, but I believe in the funk.

hostNote
Groove is one of the most elusive parts of music, and it is a **game of milliseconds**. Why some music feels good, why a bassist and drummer **lock**, and why clicking quantize is not the same job. Researchers put a 10 ms floor on what humans can hear. I feel five. Life is not a solo sport. Neither is the take. The science-of-feel stack is **prediction**, not reaction. Wait for the kick and you are already late. I talk through a statically placed groove (kick, then bass about **seven-ish milliseconds** later), **Stuart Copeland** rushing while Sting and Andy Summers reel him in, two-drummer high-wire acts (**Tedeschi Trucks** at the Rady Shell, **Kamasi Washington**), and why guitarists tend to rush while bassists drag. Last week's cluster-chord episode is the harmonic cousin of this argument. Then the papers: [**Large and Jones**](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.106.1.119) on Dynamic Attending Theory, and [**Danielsen et al.**](https://doi.org/10.1037/xhp0000611) on P-centers: the perceived timing of a note, not the visual onset in the DAW. A Splice hit that *looks* on-grid can sit 20 ms late. AI is not going anywhere, so the next phase of human music is more humanity, not more quantize. **Relationships make the groove.** I believe in the funk. Trust your ears. Don't trust your eyes.

faq
  • question
    What do I mean by groove as a game of milliseconds?
    answer
    I mean the kick-to-bass gap, the P-center of a sample, and the tiny placement choices that make a groove feel ahead, behind, or together. Researchers often put a 10 ms floor on what humans can hear. I feel five in Pro Tools, and a statically placed groove can live around seven milliseconds between kick and bass, faster than reaction time, so it has to be prediction. Quantize is not the same job as locking.
  • question
    Why do guitarists rush and bassists drag?
    answer
    A pick attack is sharp and almost immediate, so guitarists naturally sit on top of the beat. Bass strings take more energy to speak, so the note arrives later. Once you learn to delay the guitar (sit a little behind the low end), you unlock a different groove. It is mechanics plus listening, not a personality flaw.
  • question
    What is a P-center, and why shouldn't I trust the waveform?
    answer
    The perceptual center is where a sound feels like it sits in time, which is not always the visual onset in Logic or Pro Tools. Danielsen et al. (2019) found that fast, short attacks localize tightly, while slow attacks smear. A Splice kick with 20 ms of noise before the hit looks on-grid and plays late. Trust your ears. Don't trust your eyes.
  • question
    Is groove just playing behind the beat?
    answer
    No. Nudging everything back is not the point, and neither is fake humanization. Large and Jones on Dynamic Attending Theory: you feel good when events land where you predicted. Groove is a shared perceptual center where players sound like they are listening. Relationships make the groove, even if the other player is another take of you, or a grid you decided to listen to.
